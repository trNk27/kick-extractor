    How it works
    1. Find the kicks. Look for sudden jumps in the spectrum, work out the tempo, and keep only hits that sit on the beat grid. Hits with hardly any bass, like chord changes in a breakdown, are dropped.
    2. Group them. Hardstyle kicks are often re-pitched to follow the chords, and melodic sections have a softer kick under a sub bass. Hits are grouped by their spectrum, groups that end up sounding the same are merged, and the loudest consistent group (usually the drop kick) comes first.
    3. Line them up and average. Every hit in a group is shifted until it matches the others to the sample, then averaged. Anything that isn't locked to the kick (melody, vocals, screeches) cancels out.
    4. Split. In rawstyle each beat is kick, short gap, reverse bass. When that gap is there, the kick and the off-beat part are also saved separately.
    5. Snippets. The song is cut into sections from where kicks start and stop. The most typical two bars of each section become a loop; the first beats of the drop and the two bars before it become transitions.

    Limits. You get the kick as it sounds in the finished master, not the producer's dry sample. Anything that hits with the kick every single time stays in. Releasing tracks with kicks taken from someone else's song needs their permission.
